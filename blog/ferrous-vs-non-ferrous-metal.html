Ferrous vs Non-Ferrous.

Recycling Basics · 29 June 2026 · 3 min read

"Is this ferrous or non-ferrous?" is one of the most common questions we get, usually from someone holding up a piece of metal with no idea what it's made of. Here's the short version — and why it actually matters for what you get paid.

The quick test: does a magnet stick?

Ferrous metals contain iron, and a magnet will stick to almost all of them. Steel, cast iron, and most vehicle bodywork are ferrous. Non-ferrous metals — copper, aluminium, brass, lead, stainless steel (mostly), zinc — don't contain meaningful iron, and a magnet won't grab them. Keep a fridge magnet in the van; it'll settle most arguments before you even reach the yard.

Why the difference matters for price

Non-ferrous metals are generally worth far more per kilo than ferrous ones, because they're more expensive to mine and refine in the first place and the global demand for recycled copper, aluminium, and brass stays strong. Steel and other ferrous scrap is priced lower per kilo, but it's usually the bulk of any demolition or vehicle job — the volume makes up for the lower rate.

Where it gets mixed up

  • Stainless steel contains chromium and nickel and behaves like a non-ferrous metal for pricing, even though it has "steel" in the name and a magnet will sometimes weakly stick to certain grades.
  • Old appliances and motors are usually a mix of both — a steel casing wrapped around copper windings and aluminium parts. We separate these on site, so you don't need to strip them yourself.
  • Galvanised steel (zinc-coated) is still ferrous — the coating doesn't change the base metal.

If you're not sure what you've got, don't worry about sorting it perfectly before you arrive — bring it in and we'll grade it properly on the scale.
